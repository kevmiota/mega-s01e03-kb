The table counts by tenant and channel, the desk’s own view. The reports it footnotes as a one-day move are counted inside parser there, and they are the third problem below.

  • parser (#1): the shared parser reads every date month first. Our tenants write the day first, so the day and the month swap: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.”
  • hint (#2): the booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People at day-first tenants type what the hint says, and the form reads it day first. The result is also a swap, so these reports look like parser, but the parser fix does not touch them. Only reports sent through the form of a booking-v3 tenant have this cause. The table marks this row “no ticket of its own”; its ticket is #2.
  • one-day move (#3): the date moves by one day, or a meeting lands on two different days for two time zones: “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” No day and month swap here. These are the reports in desk-notes.json, footnoted under the table.

How the reports were placed

  • Each report is counted once (uniqueById). The export pages overlap, so some reports appear twice in the file.
  • parser and hint come from the tenant and the channel (causeOf in causes.ts), not from the intake tag. Intake guesses from the reporter’s browser and disagrees with the tenant on many reports.

Why it was merged as one

The dedupe rule merges on the subject line. All three problems produce the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Date is wrong”), so subject matching put them in one ticket. Decision 0001 accepted that reports with different subjects about one bug would split. It did not consider the opposite case: reports with one subject about different bugs. That case is what happened here.
